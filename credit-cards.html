How to compare credit cards

A useful credit-card comparison should look beyond rewards. Check the annual or joining fee, renewal fee, interest rate, reward structure, redemption rules, foreign transaction charges, late-payment charges and eligibility requirements.

The best card depends on how you spend. A travel-focused card may be valuable for someone who uses airport benefits and travel rewards, while a cashback card may be simpler for everyday purchases. Always read the issuer's current terms before applying.

Use offers responsibly

Credit cards are a payment and borrowing product, not free money. Paying the full statement balance on time can help avoid interest on eligible purchases, while carrying balances can become expensive. Do not choose a card only because of a welcome offer.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher annual fee mean a better card? No. Compare the fee with benefits you are realistically likely to use.

Are the offers on this site guaranteed? No. Eligibility, approval, fees and promotional offers are determined by the relevant issuer or provider and can change.
