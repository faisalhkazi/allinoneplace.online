    Why plain formatting matters

    Applicant tracking systems parse resumes as text before a person ever sees them — multi-column layouts, text boxes, icons, and tables often scramble or drop content entirely during that parsing step. A single-column layout with standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills) and plain bullet points, exactly what this tool produces, is the format that parses most reliably across the major systems.

    What makes a resume ATS-friendly?

    An applicant tracking system (ATS) is software employers may use to organize and search applications. A practical ATS-friendly resume normally uses clear section headings, readable formatting, standard job titles and text that accurately reflects the skills and experience in the job description.

    Avoid relying on images to communicate essential information. Keep dates, employers, responsibilities and achievements as selectable text. Tailor the document to the role instead of filling it with unrelated keywords.

    How to improve your resume

    Start with a concise summary, focus experience bullets on outcomes, quantify results where possible and include relevant tools or technologies. Proofread the final document and check that the exported file looks correct before sending it.

    Frequently asked questions

    Should I use the same resume for every job? A strong base resume is useful, but tailoring the summary, skills and achievement bullets to each role can make the application more relevant.

    Does ATS optimization guarantee an interview? No. ATS-friendly formatting can improve readability and parsing, but hiring decisions depend on the employer and the quality of your application.
